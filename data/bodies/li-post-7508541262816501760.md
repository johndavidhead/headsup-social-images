The University of Chicago and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign each landed a five-year, $37.5 million National Science Foundation (NSF) grant for quantum research. The grants are part of $290 million NSF put behind eight Quantum Leap Challenge Institutes, as John Pletz reported in Crain's Chicago Business. I mentioned the Illinois renewal a couple of weeks ago, but Pletz's piece has the specifics on the numbers. 

Brian DeMarco, who directs the UIUC-led Institute for Hybrid Quantum Architectures and Networks, estimates about 30 of its PhD students went to work at companies and another 30 went into faculty and national lab roles. His team has about 70 researchers, and its second phase adds 16 industry partners including Googlee, IBM, IonQ and Quantinuum. 

The UChicago-led group of about 30 researchers works on quantum sensing in biology and medicine, and Gregory Engel, who co-directs the Berggren Center for Quantum Biology and Medicine, said sensing has reached the point where it can start answering real biological questions. 

Why this matters? Add the $125 million DOE grants to Chicago-area national labs that were renewed last year and the research base behind the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park keeps getting deeper. My take is that the grants matter less than the people that they enable. Companies deciding where to build a quantum team will follow the PhDs, and Chicago is already training workers for the IQMP.
