# I said I would not bet against Chicago on quantum. July reinforced my confidence.

2026-08-12 | https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-said-would-bet-against-chicago-quantum-july-reinforced-john-head-ghyhc/

A month ago I spent a morning at the The Executives'​ Club of Chicago for The Quantum Leap event and wrote afterward that Chicago is making a serious bet on quantum and that I would not bet against it. That was how I felt at the end of a good day, not a position I had spent a lot of time digging into. Four weeks later I have more evidence than I expected to have this soon, and almost all of it landed inside a thirty day window.

Start with the result that matters most. On July 30, 2026, IBM and the University of Chicago published a quantum advantage demonstration on 70 logical qubits, running 2,415 logical two-qubit operations and 468 T gates in about fifteen minutes against classical methods that could not finish the job at all. Error correction held the logical error rates ten times below the physical rates. The part I keep coming back to is the verification, because the team proved a statistical bound on how faithfully the computation actually ran. Jay Gambetta said we are now firmly in the quantum advantage era, and I discount vendor sentences like that by half out of habit. What is left is still the first quantum result I could put in front of a CFO without a footnote asking them to trust the machine.

The money stopped being speculative a while ago

Capitol News Illinois put the public commitment at roughly $960 million, with the State of Illinois the largest funder at about $700 million and Cook County adding $20 million. Around 89% of that is equipment and infrastructure and the remaining 11% is long-term tax incentives. Private capital has moved alongside it, including $200 million from Blue Owl Capital to finance construction of PsiQuantum's facility, and total committed investment has passed $1 billion since the 2024 announcement.

Then Washington, DC bought in literally. On May 21 the Commerce Department took minority equity stakes in four companies slated to operate at the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park under the CHIPS and Science Act: $1 billion in IBM, $100 million in PsiQuantum, $100 million in Infleqtion, and $38 million in Diraq. Federal money going into quantum is not new. Federal money going in as equity, in companies clustered on one Chicago campus, is a different signal about who Washington thinks is going to build this.

The tenant list keeps filling in around them. Pasqal committed more than $65 million for its U.S. headquarters at the park. Governor Pritzker used the Global Quantum Forum stage on July 22 to announce that Zero Point Cryogenics will open its first U.S. location at the IQMP, bringing more than 20 specialized positions with an estimated $1.8 million in state tax credits through the MICRO program. Those are small numbers next to the billions already committed here and I think they are the more interesting ones, because Zero Point hand-builds dilution refrigerators and every superconducting quantum computer lives inside one. Illinois also put up a $3 million fund in July to pull NSF X-Labs quantum teams into the state. Buying the supply chain and the talent pipeline is a less glamorous strategy than buying qubits, and it is the one that compounds.

All of this sits on 128 acres of the former U.S. Steel South Works site on the Southeast Side, dormant for more than thirty years. The target completion is 2028, and the state projects up to $60 billion in economic impact.

The proof is already running on Chicago fiber

Construction photos are easy. Production traffic is not. On August 1, 2026, Northwestern University researchers sent entangled photons 24 kilometers through live Chicago internet fiber, sharing the same strands as ordinary traffic. Three days later Quantum Corridor completed a 1.6 Tb/s quantum-safe encrypted link between data centers in Chicago and Hammond, Indiana, on its production network, pairing Ciena WaveLogic 6 Extreme optical encryption running NIST-certified post-quantum algorithms with Toshiba quantum key distribution servers. Ciena says its existing WaveLogic 5e customers can add post-quantum support through a software upgrade, which quietly turns a budget objection into a change control question.

I have been selling technology in this city long enough to know the difference between a demo and a deployment. Those two are deployments.

Where this crosses the enterprise AI work I do every day

Last week I sat in on the The Economic Club of Chicago Member Exchange inside the University of Chicago's Hyde Park Labs , where Nadya Mason made the case that quantum will complement high performance computing rather than replace it, because its real strength is optimization problems carrying more variables than a classical machine can hold. She expects quantum sensing to reach commercial products first, inside a couple of years, since that needs nanoscale devices rather than a full fault-tolerant computer. Her cryptography point is the one that stuck with me, and it is the same one Vincent J. Beltrani and Mekena McGrew, PhD made at the Exec Club in July: people are harvesting encrypted data today so they can read it the moment a quantum computer can break it.

That is why I keep putting quantum and AI in the same conversation with clients instead of treating them as separate roadmaps. The quantum processors are going to sit next to the GPUs in the same hybrid stack our AI workloads already run on. The proficiency curve is the one we lived through with data science and then again with AI, roughly two years to get a team competent, and commercially relevant quantum use cases are 18 to 36 months out. Any executive who reads those two numbers together can see that waiting for the hardware means starting two years behind.

The algorithm gap got an institution in July as well. The National Quantum Algorithm Center, announced by IBM and the State of Illinois back in December 2024, became an operating membership organization at the Global Quantum Forum, with early focus on clean energy and drug chemistry simulation and $125,000 Grand Challenges awards behind researchers working those problems. Hardware progress has outrun the supply of people who can turn a business problem into a quantum circuit, and Chicago now has an institution whose entire job is closing that distance.

The part I am still cautious about

PsiQuantum anchors the park and sits in Stage C of Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA)'s Quantum Benchmarking Initiative, the final verification phase, without a public logical qubit on the architecture being evaluated. Quantinuum , IBM, QuEra Computing Inc. and IonQ sit a stage behind in Stage B with stronger public results, and their Stage C decisions land late this year. I expect to hear Stage C cited around town as validation. It is admission to the exam rather than a passing grade, and I would rather say that out loud than have a client hear it somewhere else.

Workforce is the other open question. Chicago State's summer quantum program took 12 students out of 80 applicants, so demand is already running past supply. PsiQuantum put $250,000 into South Chicago education on August 3, funding George Washington and St. Francis De Sales high schools, Olive-Harvey College, Chicago State's CQuEST center, and community groups including Project SYNCERE. Local organizations have asked for a community benefits agreement requiring that 25% of full-time hires in the first three years come from surrounding neighborhoods. Whether that number gets honored will tell you more about what this campus does for Chicago than any qubit count will.

What I would recommend one does with quantum now

There are two moves I would consider right now, and neither one requires a quantum strategy.

Inventory your cryptography and give it an owner with a name. Most enterprises I work with cannot answer what algorithms protect their most sensitive data or where those keys live, and quantum-safe standards are already published, so this is a migration project you can scope today.

Then pick one real problem and run quantum-inspired methods against it on the CPUs and GPUs you already own. Fraud detection results improving by 20% or more and sharper Monte Carlo simulations behind financial risk models are available now, and the work teaches your people quantum principles while it pays for itself.

Chicago spent thirty years looking at a dormant steel site on the Southeast Side. Now it has a federal equity stake, an IBM quantum advantage result with a University of Chicago name on it, entangled photons moving across the same fiber that carries our email, and a fabricator of dilution refrigerators moving in down the block. I was cautious about this in July. I am not now.

If you are running an enterprise AI program in this city, what would it take to get quantum onto the same roadmap this year, and who on your team would own it?
