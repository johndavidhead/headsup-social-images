# Bank of America: $400M in AI, $800M back

2026-10-13 | https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7515789361272025091/

Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan told a Barclays investor conference in September that about 140 AI uses cost the bank $400 million and are generating $800 million in benefit.

The bank now expects to double its AI budget next year, and CTO Hari Gopalkrishnan said it plainly in an interview: "We will probably end up spending twice next year [what] we did this year." One of those uses is EricaAssist, which 18,000 Bank of America service reps use to cut almost a minute off each customer call.

The thing I keep coming back to is that Moynihan framed AI the way a CFO frames any capital project, with dollars in and dollars out. PYMNTS reports European banks are starting to talk about AI the same way, as savings targets on the cost line.

My take for a CEO, CFO or board: if your AI business cases still lead with hours saved, expect the next question to be the benefit number. A 2 to 1 return on $400 million is now a public benchmark your peers can point to.

What would change in your AI budget review if every use case had to show its benefit in dollars?
