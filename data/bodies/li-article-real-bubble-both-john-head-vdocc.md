1. The Move That Mattered

The bubble question finally got a straight answer.

I put my own view on paper during the last couple weeks. Is the AI economy real, or a bubble? The honest answer is both. AI infrastructure is running at roughly a $175B annualized rate, which is real demand and real money. It is also about 0.42% of US GDP, which is small next to the volume of the noise. The spending is real. The returns mostly are not, yet.

Why it matters: the bubble talk is a distraction from the question that pays your bills. Not "will this pop." The question is whether your AI program returns a dollar. Stop watching the macro chart. Go audit whether one thing you funded this year actually earns its cost.

2. What's Actually Shipping

Blink the past couple weeks and you missed three enterprise deployments worth studying. The vendors stopped selling models and started selling outcomes, and they are putting their own people inside your building to get there.

AWS moved into the building. Amazon Web Services committed $1B to embed forward-deployed engineers inside enterprise clients. That is a vendor betting that the hard part is your workflow, not their model.

Capita cut screening time 43%. Capita launched its Forward Deployed Orchestrator and cut candidate screening time 43% in the first four months. That is a production number, not a pilot slide.

ServiceNow scaled the agents. ServiceNow pushed its Autonomous Workforce into every major business function, and banks moved agentic AI from pilot to real scale with double-digit productivity gains. HP signed a Frontier partnership with OpenAI to rewire its operations, and IBM paired with OpenAI to bring frontier models into enterprise security.

The pattern: 2025 was pilots. 2026 is all about implementation.

3. The Risk I'm Watching

Your next wave of AI value is gated by data controls, not model quality.

Stanford's 2026 AI Index landed on a number worth sitting with. 62% of organizations now cite security and risk as the top barrier to scaling agentic AI. That beats technical limits and regulatory uncertainty by 24 points. Model maturity stopped being the hard part a while ago. The thing holding agents back is that enterprises cannot govern the data access an autonomous agent needs, so the agents stay parked.

The numbers from the index:

62% cite security and risk as the top barrier to scaling agents
That lead is 24 points ahead of the next barrier
88% of companies now use AI in at least one function
Agent deployment still sits in the single digits almost everywhere

What to do Monday: audit your data access rules for software that acts on its own, before you green-light the next agent. The model you pick matters less than the policies around it.

4. The Model Layer Price War

The model layer turned into a price war in a single week. Four moves, and they all point at the same thing: capability is a commodity, and the buy is what changes.

OpenAI shipped GPT-5.6. It came with a three-tier enterprise strategy. The vendor is segmenting the purchase, which means procurement now picks a tier, not just a model.

Google cleared Gemini 3.5 Pro. It is cleared for a July enterprise launch. Another frontier option lands in the enterprise catalog this month.

Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5. It runs at near Opus-level performance for roughly 40 to 60% less per token, generally available in GitHub Copilot on day one. Price competition is now the headline, not raw capability.

Fable 5 came back online. It returned after a 19-day export-control shutdown, with a new safeguard in place. Model supply carries policy risk, not only technical risk, and your continuity plan should say so.

Translate it for the roadmap: the model is a fast-moving commodity with real price competition. The advantage that lasts is deployment, who gets these systems into production and proves the outcome.

5. The Human Layer

Two reports landed the same week, and read together they say the gap is people and process, not models.

Bain says AI budgets keep climbing while the returns lag. A separate survey of roughly 6,000 executives found more than 80% report no measurable productivity gains from AI so far. A third of leaders use AI themselves, but only about 90 minutes a week. That is not a model problem. The models are already good enough. The gap is process redesign and adoption. The companies seeing returns rebuilt the workflow around the AI. Everyone else bolted AI onto the old process and asked why nothing moved.

The question to sit with: if your AI budget is up and your P&L is flat, a bigger model will not fix it. Pick one messy workflow and rebuild it end to end until the number moves.
