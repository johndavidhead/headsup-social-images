Last week, I sat in on the The Economic Club of Chicago's Member Exchange at The Chicago School's new offices, and within a few minutes Dr. Michele Nealon, Psy.D. had a room full of executives standing up and twirling each other. Seeing many of my ECC standing and dancing with each other were moments full of laughter. Besides kicking off the event with humor, Dr. Nealon's point was that a leader's mood spreads through an organization almost immediately, so asking yourself whether you are happy is part of the job. 

Dr. Manika M. Turnbull, PhD, MBA, Chief People Officer at Health Care Service Corporation, said she is happiest when her team has room to disagree and then "stacks hands" on one decision. Dr. Michael Horowitz, president of The Community Solution Education System, calls his version radical cooperation across six universities, where nobody has to agree walking in the door but everyone works toward the same outcome. 

The part that stuck with me most was Manika asking herself whether a decision has had enough "soap time," because under stress her default is to jump straight to solution mode. Michele's rule for a hard call is to ask three people, and one of them has to be a deliberate contrarian who won't tell her what she wants to hear. I have watched plenty of executives, myself included, skip the contrarian when the pressure is highest. Manika also tells herself "fix your face" before she walks into a room with her team, which was the most practical advice I heard all month. 

An audience member brought up the Financial Times piece on stress among people building AI at the leading labs, and the panel's answer was to connect people to the purpose of the work and get behind the real worry, which is usually about their job and identity. 

Thank you David Snyder and the club for such a thought provoking event.
