# Bill Gates sees AI cyberattack surprises within a year

2026-10-12 | https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7515426458283085824/

Bill Gates told Jake Tapper on CNN's State of the Union on Sunday that the AI risk he expects to hit hardest in the next year is cyberattacks. "When you have bad people, no safeguards, and then the latest models, that's where, over the next year," he said, "primarily in the cyber area, we are going to have a lot of negative surprises."

Gates laid out the business model plainly: threaten a company and ask it to pay, or cause a disruption that moves the stock market. He called AI getting out of control a problem that is years away. CrowdStrike's 2026 Global Threat Report backs up his near-term worry, with attacks by AI-enabled adversaries up 89% in 2025 and the average time for attackers to move laterally inside a network down to 29 minutes.

The thing I keep coming back to is the 29 minutes. An incident response plan that waits on a person to approve each step was built for a slower attacker than the one Gates is describing.

For a CEO or board, my take is to move AI-enabled extortion to the top of the 12-month risk list and run a tabletop exercise at that speed before the year is out.

When did your team last test its response plan against an attacker moving that fast, and what did you change after?
