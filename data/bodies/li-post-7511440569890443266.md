# This week at the The Economic Club of Chicago I sat in the Harris Theater for Music and Dance and listened to...

2026-10-01 | https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7511440569890443266/

This week at the The Economic Club of Chicago I sat in the Harris Theater for Music and Dance and listened to David Axelrod and Karl Rove talk through the 2026 midterms, with PBS NewsHour's Lisa Desjardins moderating. 

What made it worth the afternoon is that both of them ran national campaigns and worked inside the White House, Axelrod for President Obama and Rove for President George W. Bush. Axelrod opened with history: only two presidents since World War II saw their party gain seats in a midterm, Clinton in 1998 and Bush in 2002, and he jokingly gave Rove full credit for the second one. Both expect Democrats to take the House, and both expect a modest margin because redistricting has shrunk the number of competitive seats, with Axelrod guessing a 15-seat majority and Rove putting Democrats at 225 to 230 seats. The Senate is where they split. Axelrod called Alaska, Iowa, Ohio and Texas coin flips, while Rove went state by state from Susan Collins in Maine to Michele Tafoya in Minnesota. 

On election security they disagreed openly, with Axelrod laying out the scenarios that worry him and Rove explaining why state-run elections make them unlikely, and the exchange stayed civil the whole way. Both men have strong opinions, but it was nice to see people with different viewpoints banter and debate. We need much more of that in these current times.
