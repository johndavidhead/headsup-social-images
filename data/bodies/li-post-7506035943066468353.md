# EY's new AI Risk and Governance Survey covered 202 senior AI executives at companies with at least $1 billion...

2026-09-16 | https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7506035943066468353/

EY's new AI Risk and Governance Survey covered 202 senior AI executives at companies with at least $1 billion in revenue, and 91% of them now run agentic AI. The one data point that jumped off the page for me is that 98% have formal governance policies while 26% cannot detect unauthorized AI agents running inside their own company. Nearly half admit the governance process was skipped for at least one urgent deployment. More than a third have already had an AI incident cause material harm, including data loss or financial damage. 

The finding I find most useful is what happened when companies actually ran a formal assurance review: 64% ended up significantly modifying a quarter or more of their AI systems. This is a known problem. I see the skipped-process pattern constantly with large companies, where the governance board meets monthly and the business deploys weekly. A policy that gets bypassed under deadline pressure is a suggestion, and the EY data says almost half of $1 billion companies are running on suggestions. My advice to leaders is to run one  review on the agents already in production before approving any new ones, since the EY numbers say you will find something worth fixing. Most won't stop the implementation process going forward but it's worth the effort to review what's running today.

When was the last time your company reviewed the agents already running, and what did you change as a result?
