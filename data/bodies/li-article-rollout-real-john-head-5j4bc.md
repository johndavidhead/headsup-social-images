2. Microsoft and Big Tech Watch

Four moves in two weeks, and they all point at the same thing: the agent era is being priced, governed, and shipped right now.

Build 2026 made agents employees. Microsoft spent its keynote telling enterprises that agents are about to act like employees, and that almost nobody is ready. Agent Mode becomes the default in Word, Excel, Teams, and Outlook later this month. Microsoft Copilot Studio shipped agents that click through websites and desktop apps the way a person does, reaching old line-of-business systems that never had an API. Agent365 is the control pane that inventories and governs them through Entra, Defender, and Purview, and it syncs with Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Google agent registries. The model is becoming a commodity you route to. The value, and the risk, now sit in the layer that watches what hundreds of agents do with your data.

Work IQ APIs ship June 16. IT teams can now build agents grounded in Microsoft 365 context, tools, and controls, with Copilot Studio 2.0 and an Agent Analytics dashboard tracking cost and accuracy. The vendor question moves from whose model wins this month to whose platform plugs into your data with real controls. For most F500 buyers the M365 graph is already the system of record, which hands Microsoft a head start as the default grounding layer for production agents. Your AI vendor decision is starting to look a lot like your data strategy.

Autopilots are a new category. Copilot waits to be asked. Autopilots do not. Microsoft Scout, the first one, monitors email, Teams, calendar, OneDrive, and SharePoint around the clock and acts on your behalf, with its own governed Entra identity tied to IT policy. It is built on OpenClaw, Microsoft's open-source agent framework, and is rolling out to Frontier organizations in private preview. This is the shift from AI that helps you work to AI that works while you do other things.

Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 4.8. Released May 28. It scores 69.2% on SWE-bench Pro, is four times less likely to leave code flaws unreported, and pricing held at $5/$25 per million tokens. Fable 5, released this week, is even more accurate. And costs double as Opus. The feature to study is Effort Control: four levels that let you dial how hard the model thinks. For teams running Claude in production it is a cost lever. Route routine tasks to low, reserve max for the work that deserves it, and cut token spend without giving up quality where it counts.

3. The Risk I'm Watching

Your AI budget line item is about to move, and not in your favor.

$29 a month to $750. That is what some developers project their GitHub Copilot bill becomes under the token billing that went live June 1. Agentic sessions burn tokens fast. Power users report projected increases of 10x to 50x, and the fallback model is gone. Code completions stay unlimited, so casual users will barely notice. The teams that actually operationalized agents are the ones holding the bill.

Here is the pattern to internalize. Microsoft trained the market on flat-rate AI, then moved the meter to usage right as agents made usage explode. Flat-rate AI pricing was a customer acquisition cost, and it is ending across the industry. Every vendor watched this play.

What to do Monday: re-run your FY27 AI budget with usage-based pricing scenarios, and put a consumption clause review on every AI renewal that crosses your desk this quarter.

4. The Human Layer

Three of the most-cited enterprise AI reports dropped in the same window. Read together, they say the gap is people and process, not models.

McKinsey & Company : the scaling gap is the whole story. 62% of organizations are experimenting with or piloting AI agents, but no more than 10% have scaled them in any single function. Only 23% report scaling agentic AI anywhere, and roughly 1% call their AI strategy mature. Eight in ten point to data limitations as the roadblock. The constraint moved off the model and onto the operating model.

Deloitte : more momentum, same catch. 3,235 leaders across 24 countries. 66% already see real productivity gains, worker access to AI jumped 50% in 2025, and the share of firms with 40% or more of their AI projects in production is set to double within six months. One report reads as "most of you are stuck at pilot." The other reads as "the leaders are about to pull away." Both are true.

WRITER : the adoption strain is real. 79% of organizations are struggling with adoption, up double digits from 2025, and 54% of C-suite leaders say the rollout is straining their companies. Super-users save nine hours a week, 4.5x more than laggards. Meanwhile 92% of executives say they are building an "AI elite" and 60% plan to push out non-adopters. When a workforce divides into two tiers, the cause is usually thin enablement, not a missing tool.

The question to ask: are you tracking who actually gets value from AI, or just counting licenses?

5. The Quantum Window

Classical computers are light bulbs. Quantum computers are lasers. Both make light. Neither replaces the other.

That was the best analogy from The Quantum Leap at The Executives'​ Club of Chicago , where I spent a morning across four sessions. The takeaways for enterprise leaders: near-term wins are in optimization, fraud detection, and simulation. Commercial use cases are 18 to 36 months out. Proficiency takes about two years to build, and that window is open now. Quantum-inspired algorithms running on today's CPUs and GPUs already improve fraud detection by 20% or more, and quantum-safe encryption standards exist today.

Chicago is making a serious bet. Construction is underway at the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park , IBM is modeling large molecules with hybrid quantum-classical methods, and Inflection AI, a local quantum startup, just went public at $3 billion. The workforce gap is wider than the technology gap: Chicago State University's summer quantum program took 12 students out of 80 applicants.

For those of us in enterprise AI, this all rhymes. The two-year proficiency curve is what we saw with data science and again with AI. Companies that waited started two years behind. Same playbook: build skills early and plan the encryption migration before you need it.
