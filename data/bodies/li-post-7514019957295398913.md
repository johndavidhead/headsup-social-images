# I heard Patagonia CEO Ryan Gellert at the The Economic Club of Chicago yesterday, and his thoughts on AI are...

2026-10-08 | https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7514019957295398913/

I heard Patagonia CEO Ryan Gellert at the The Economic Club of Chicago yesterday, and his thoughts on AI are what made a lasting impression. He told the room he is not bullish on AI for humanity, compared it directly to social media, and said he has no confidence in the frontier lab leaders or the administration to set the pace. He also said it would be irresponsible not to give Patagonias 3,500 employees access to it.

Where he plans to put real resources is inventory, which he called Patagonia's biggest cost center and its biggest environmental footprint. For me, that is the most honest enterprise AI use case I have heard from a CEO this year, because it is measurable and it is exactly what machine learning does well. Better inventory performance on the 30 million units Patagonia makes each year helps the P&L and the end-of-life problem he openly admitted the company has not solved. He paired it with a resale milestone: starting next week, Patagonia.com will show used and new products side by side and let you buy both in the same cart.

The lesson learned for anyone working in tech is that you can carry real doubts about AI and still find the one place in the business where it pays for itself.
